Performance & Learning, in plain English

A no-jargon walk-through of what Performance Appraisal is, where you step in to build the Learning & Development module, what L&D adds, and how the two connect. Read this first — then the detailed specs will make sense.

01

The whole thing in one sentence

Performance measures how well people did their jobs this year; that result decides who needs what development; and Learning & Development (the part you're building) actually delivers that development and proves it worked.

Think of it as a relay race. Performance runs the first leg and hands a baton — “here's who needs to grow, and in what” — to Learning & Development, which runs the next leg. Your job is to build the runner that catches the baton and finishes the race.

02

What is Performance Appraisal?

It's the bank's yearly way of answering one question: “Did this employee do what the bank needs them to do?” To keep it fair and consistent, everyone's work is broken down the same way:

Perspectivethe “area”
A big bucket of focus. The bank uses four: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth. Each carries a weight (e.g. Financial = 35%).
Objectivethe “goal”
A specific goal inside a perspective — e.g. “Grow deposit mobilisation.”
KPI / Measurethe “ruler”
How you measure the goal — e.g. “Total deposits (GHS).” This is what gets scored.
Plain version: Perspective = the subject (like “Maths”). Objective = the topic (“Algebra”). KPI = the test score that proves you learned it. Weights just say which subjects matter most.
03

How a performance year actually runs

Four simple stages, start to finish:

1

Set goals (at the start of the year)

The employee proposes their goals for each perspective; the weights must add up to 100%. Their line manager approves them.

Employee → Manager
2

Do the work (during the year)

The employee works toward those goals. Nothing fancy here — this is just the year happening.

Employee
3

Appraise (at the end of the year)

Two people score the same goals: the employee rates themselves, then the manager rates them. It's a “two-in-one” — self-assessment plus manager assessment.

Self + Manager
4

Get a score

The scores roll up into one number (out of 5) and a rating. This score is the baton. It decides what happens next.

Final score 1–5
04

What the score triggers

The appraisal doesn't just sit there. The final score sends the employee down one of three paths — this is the moment Performance starts talking to development:

< 3

Struggling

Goes onto a PIP (Performance Improvement Plan) — a structured plan with activities and check-ins to get back on track.
3–4

Solid

Continues on a normal development plan (IDP) to keep growing in the role.
4+

Star

Flagged for the leadership pipeline and given priority for development resources.

Notice that all three paths are about development. That's the doorway into your module.

05

Where you come in

Before the bank can deliver any development, every employee writes an Individual Development Plan (IDP) — “here are my growth goals for the year and how I'll achieve them.” Each goal lists activities, and each activity is tagged with a learning method using the 70-20-10 rule:

70%on-the-job
Learning by doing — stretch assignments, job rotation, leading a project.
20%social
Learning from others — mentoring, coaching, peer groups, 360° feedback.
10%formal
Structured learning — courses, certifications, workshops, e-learning. This is the slice L&D mostly sources and pays for.

📍 This is your starting line

Performance + IDP already exist in the system. You pick up where the IDP ends. Every employee's plan says what training they want — your L&D module collects all of those, turns them into real programs, gets people onto them, and measures the result.

06

What the L&D module adds

Today the IDP just records what people want. L&D is everything that turns that wish-list into delivered, proven learning:

  • Needs assessment

    Gathers training needs from three places: bank-wide mandatory programs, department requests, and the individual IDPs.

  • Program catalog & setup

    Creates the actual courses — dates, providers, and costs (in multiple currencies).

  • Enrollment & notifications

    Puts the right people on the right programs (checking they're not on leave or double-booked) and tells them automatically.

  • Evaluation

    Measures whether learning happened (pre/post test), got applied (action plan signed off by the manager), and had business impact.

  • Budget & reporting

    Tracks spend against budget and answers “how many trained, at what cost, with what result?” instantly.

07

How the two link

The connection is mostly data handed from Performance into L&D. You don't rebuild any of this — you read it:

From Performance →L&D uses it to…
IDP training choicesAggregate everyone's “formal training” picks into the needs assessment and program demand.
Appraisal scorePrioritise who gets development first — pipeline (4+) ahead, PIP (under 3) for remediation.
IDP coaching / mentoringSet up and oversee the coaching pairings (the 20% social-learning slice).
Action plan + manager sign-offFeed L&D's evaluation — proof the learning was applied on the job.
Employee, grade & department (Core HR)Decide eligibility, cost-per-grade, and run reports by department.
The one rule to remember: Performance produces the demand (“who needs what”). L&D produces the supply (“here's the program, you're enrolled, here's the proof it worked”).
08

One person's journey (worked example)

Follow Ama, a Relationship Manager, through both modules:

PERF
Jan: Ama sets her goals — “grow deposits by 10%” (Financial), “improve CSAT” (Customer). Her manager approves.
PERF
Dec: Ama and her manager appraise the year. Final score: 3.6 / 5 — solid, with a gap in data analysis.
IDP
Dec: In her development plan she adds a goal: “Strengthen credit analysis,” with a formal-training activity — “complete a credit certification.”
L&D
Jan (your module): Ama's certification request lands in needs assessment alongside 40 others asking for the same thing. L&D sets up the program, costs it, and enrols Ama — checking she isn't on leave.
L&D
Mar: Ama gets an automatic invite, accepts, sits a pre-course self-assessment, attends, then a post-course one — the jump proves she learned.
L&D
Jun: Ama's action plan (“apply credit skills to 3 live deals”) is signed off by her manager. L&D records the business impact and the cost — and it all shows on the dashboard.
