How Performance feeds Learning

A plain-English guide to one journey that crosses two teams: HR & managers run performance and produce the demand for development; the L&D team turns that into real programs and proves they worked — the supply. This shows exactly where each of you fits.

01

The whole thing in one line

Performance answers “how did people do, and what do they need to grow?” — then hands that list to Learning & Development, which answers “here's the training, you're booked on it, and here's proof it worked.”
02

Two halves, one baton

Think of a relay race. The Performance half is already live in the system and owned by HR and line managers. The Learning & Development half is owned by the L&D team. The baton passed between them is simple: “who needs to grow, and in what.”

Performance

Already built · runs yearly

Goals are set, the year is appraised, a score comes out, and every employee writes a development plan (IDP) that lists the training they need.

Owned by HR & line managers

Learning & Development

Being built · the next leg

Collects everyone's training needs, builds & costs the programs, books the right people, and measures whether the learning happened and got used.

Owned by the L&D team
03

Where do I fit?

Find your lane. You only need to do your part — the system carries the information across for you.

If you're in HR or a manager

The Performance side
  • Agree each person's goals at the start of the year and approve them.
  • Run the appraisal — the employee self-rates, the manager rates, scores are moderated for fairness.
  • Help each person write a development plan (IDP) — the growth goals and the training they need.
  • The score routes people: under 3 → improvement plan; 3–4 → keep growing; 4+ → leadership pipeline.
  • That's your handoff — you don't book or pay for training. You produce the demand.

If you're in L&D

The Learning side
  • See every plan gathered into a needs assessment (bank-wide, department, and individual/IDP).
  • Build the real programs — dates, providers and cost (even across currencies).
  • Enroll the right people; the system warns if someone's on leave or double-booked.
  • Evaluate: a before/after check shows learning happened; the manager signs off it was applied.
  • Answer “who, how many, what cost, what result?” instantly on the dashboard.

06

The rules that keep it real

These are the principles that make this a proper corporate system, not another spreadsheet:

One source of truth

Every plan, program, cost and result lives in one system — no parallel Excel sheets or email chains.

Demand vs. supply

HR produces the demand (“who needs what”). L&D produces the supply (“booked, delivered, proven”). Neither rebuilds the other.

No shopping lists

A training need states the problem to solve and a priority — not an open-ended wish-list of courses.

Check before booking

The system flags if someone is on leave, already trained recently, or busy elsewhere — before they're enrolled again.

Proof, not attendance

Success isn't “turned up.” It's a measured before/after and a manager's sign-off that it was used on the job.

Right people, right view

Each role sees only what it should — employee, manager, HR business partner, L&D admin — across the whole flow.
